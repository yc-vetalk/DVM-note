## p.16

Principles of Asepsis and Antisepsis
There are 4 main determinants for a surgical site infection: host 
defense, physiologic derangement, bacterial contamination risk at 
surgery, and prolonged surgical time. Other factors that impact 
infection of deep structures and organs include hypoalbuminemia 
and a prior operation.

## p.26

Anesthesia and Sedation
– 25 –
Cattle are ruminants with a very large rumen in their abdominal 
cavity. Therefore, prolonged recumbency under general anesthesia 
can easily cause bloating (also happened postoperatively), 
regurgitaion, and aspiration pneumonia. It can also interfere with 
diaphragmatic movement, leading to hypoventilation, hypoxia, 
hypercarbia, and respiratory acidosis.

## p.27

Anesthesia and Sedation

and placement of a cuffed endotracheal tube will

may be placed to facilitate displacement of gas 
and liquid contents to reduce bloat.

Use of α-2 adrenoceptor agonist, such as xylazine hydrochloride,

## p.33

Sedation
– 32 –
The effects of the sedative xylazine can be suppressed by 
antagonists such as yohimbine (0.12 mg/kg IV) or tolazoline (2–4 
mg/kg IV). Side effects of these drugs include hypertension and

## p.34

Infiltration / Regional analgesia
– 33 –
Regional analgesia results form desensitization of sensory nerve 
to a given area. This may be performed by infiltration into the 
desired location of by blocking sensory nerve innervating a region. 
Depending on the required duration of anesthesia, local anesthetic 
agents including lidocaine (short onset and duration), mepivacaine 
(rapid onset with intermediate duration, bupivacaine (long onset and 
duration) may be used.

## p.35

Infiltration / Regional analgesia
– 34 –
Many active ingredients (core structures) of anesthetics are 
organic weak bases, which are poorly water-soluble and unstable. 
Converting them into hydrochloride salts (HCl) improves water 
solubility, enhances absorption and bioavailability, and stabilizes the 
chemical structure (extending shelf life). However, because these 
aqueous solutions are slightly acidic, they can cause temporary pain 
and a burning sensation at the injection site.

## p.36

Infiltration / Regional analgesia
– 35 –
Due to cardiovascular toxicity with vascular absorption, 
bupivacaine use is usually limited to epidural and perineural 
administration. Lidocaine and mepivacaine may be used by any 
route.

## p.37

Use of Lidocaine
– 36 –
The safe total dose of lidocaine is usually 3–5 mg/kg (90–150 
mL of 2% lidocaine/600 kg cattle). Do not exceed the dose at one 
time. Lidocaine takes effect about 5–10 minutes after injection, and 
the analgesic effect lasts for about 90–180 minutes. Clinically, about 
100 mL of 2% lidocaine is sufficient for general surgery on an adult 
cow.

## p.43

Infiltration Anesthesia
– 42 –
Inject approximately 3–5 mL of lidocaine at the uppermost point 
of the incision using an 18 G needle. Switch to a 15 G, 14-cm long 
needle. Slightly elevate the skin and advance the long needle 
vertically downward into the subcutis until the entire needle is 
submerged. Slowly inject the drug while concurrently retracting the 
needle to ensure a linear distribution along the incision site. Total 
volume of anesthetic used per layer should be about 25–30 mL.

## p.44

Infiltration Anesthesia
– 43 –
Upon completion of subcutaneous anesthesia, hold the long 
needle horizontally and insert it approximately 1–1.5 cm into the 
muscular layer. Then, advance the needle vertically downward 
through the muscle layer until the entire needle is submerged. Inject 
the anesthetic while concurrently withdrawing the needle, using the 
same technique described above.

## p.45

Infiltration Anesthesia
– 44 –
Two muscular layers must be anesthetized: the first layer can 
usually be confirmed as the external abdominal oblique muscle, 
while the second layer is less distinguishable and may include the 
internal abdominal oblique, transversus abdominis, and peritoneum.
