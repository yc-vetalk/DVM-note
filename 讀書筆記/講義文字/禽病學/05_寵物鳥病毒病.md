## p.7

Affected bird species
• PDD-like features have been reported in >80 avian species. 
• African grey parrots (Psittacus erithacus), macaws (Ara 
species), Amazon parrots (Amazona species), and cockatoos
(Cacatua species) are the most common psittacine species 
affected.
• The disease is minimally represented in Quaker parrots 
(Myiopsitta monachus) and lovebird species (Agapornis).

## p.8

Transmission
• Fecal-oral, fecal-oronasal, urinary transmission
• Vertical transmission
• 2 out 30 embryos (brain, proventriculus) from PaBV-infected flocks 
were detected positive. (Lierz et al., 2011)
• 4 out of 4 embryos (brain, liver, eyes) were detected positive for viral 
RNA. (Kerski et al., 2012)
• Persistent infection
• 29 out of 31 (94%) macaws which survived an outbreak showed the 
presence of virus. (Kloet et al., 2011)
• IV or IC infection induced persistent infection up to 33 weeks. 
(Piepenbring et al., 2012)

## p.18

Prevention and control
• No vaccines available
• Antibody does not seem to be able to clear the virus.
• Studies on the subunit or attenuated vaccines are ongoing.
• Cross-genotype protection?
• No antivirals available
• drug under investigation: Ribavirin, Favipiravir
18
(Mirosseini et al., 2011; Obert et al., 2016; 
Runge et al., 2017; Takunaga et al., 2017)

## p.19

Treatment
• Quarantine (foreign à domestic)
• Disinfection
• Supportive care with fluids, antibiotics, and feeding small 
amounts of food at each meal 
• With patience, perseverance, prolonged therapy, reduced 
stress, and attention to correction of secondary problems, 
quality and longevity of life in many PaBV clinically affected 
birds can be significantly improved.
